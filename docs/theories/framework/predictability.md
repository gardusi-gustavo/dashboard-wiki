# 🎲 Predictability — the prevention illusion

**What this is:** why perfect prevention requires perfect prediction, why prediction collapses at the
combination layer, and why the only alternatives are handcuffs or accepting a world with free will.
**Metaphor only.** Pairs with [free-will.md](free-will.md), [fair-game.md](../justice/fair-game.md),
and [security.md](../fundamentals/security.md).

---

## 🔒 The handcuff limit

To guarantee that no bad thing ever happens, you need **perfect predictability** — every move known
before it is made. The logical end state is a person in handcuffs, or a total-surveillance cell.

That is not a society; it is a prison or a factory line. **Freedom is the risk.** A system that
removes all risk removes the thing it was protecting.

---

## 🎮 Move sets and combos

A person is a **Mortal Kombat character**: a small set of standard moves, a longer list of combos,
and rare moments of a new trick.

- **Standard moves** are predictable — habits, emotional defaults, cultural scripts.
- **Combos** are harder — sequences, context, and timing.
- **Novel tricks** are rare, but they are where models fail.

You can put each possibility in a category and predict the **label** of the next move — violence,
harm, help, retreat. You cannot reliably price the **combination**: donate money to a homeless person,
then hit that person. The label flips inside one sequence.

---

## 🧩 Categories vs combinations

<!-- begin table -->
| Layer | Catches | Misses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rules** | Known acts with a stated price | Acts nobody wrote down |
| **Categories** | The likely label — violent, risky, kind | The order that turns kind into harmful |
| **Monitoring** | The last visible use | The unmonitored room, the deleted log |
| **Reflex** | The trained response | The first time the situation is new |
<!-- end table -->

The model is not reading truth; it is reading the **absence of change**. Same input, different
output, the moment the state changes.

---

## 👁️ Monitoring still loses

Even a total-stream world has an enforcement gap:

- There are not enough officers to prevent everything — roughly one per 10k civilians.
- The watcher can see and do nothing — corruption, or simply being out of range.
- Enforcement arrives **after** the irreversible damage; prevention is a **before** lever.

So the system falls back on **consequences**: price the act, catch what it can, and accept the tail it
cannot catch. That is [fair-game.md](../justice/fair-game.md), not a prevention machine.
