# 🚿 Cold shower
> **Updated:** 2026-11-02

**What this is:** a short cold exposure at the end of a normal shower — a
wake-up and discipline habit, not a medical treatment.

## Protocol

- Normal warm shower first.
- End with 30–60 s cold; build to 2–3 min.
- Breathe slowly; do not gasp or hyperventilate.
- Stop before shivering or numbness.
- Dry and warm up.
- The first 1–2 weeks are harder; adaptation is expected.

## Temperature

| Water | Sensation | Use |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 25 °C+ | cool | not a cold exposure; summer tap may sit here |
| 20–25 °C | cool/cold | entry level; daily summer minimum |
| 15–20 °C | cold | target for the daily habit |
| 10–15 °C | very cold | advanced; short exposure |
| <10 °C | icy | brief only; skip in winter |

- **20 °C is a reasonable starting target.** Measure with a thermometer rather
  than guessing.
- Summer tap water is often above 20 °C. Use the coldest setting; if it stays
  above ~24 °C, it is a cool rinse, not a cold challenge.
- Do not chase below 10 °C. Consistency matters more than suffering.

## Seasons

| Season | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Summer (Dec–Mar) | Aim for every day; the streak matters |
| Autumn / Spring | 3–5×/week; optional |
| Winter (Jun–Sep) | Optional; skip if the water is below 10 °C or the room is cold |

## Streak

- Summer streak = one cold exposure per day.
- A missed day is data, not failure; resume the next day.
- Do not extend the streak with icy water, illness, or alcohol.

## Safety

- Cold shock raises heart rate and blood pressure. If you have a heart
  condition, high blood pressure, or another medical concern, ask a clinician
  before starting.
- Do not do it after alcohol, during a fever, or when feeling faint.
- Stop before shivering or numbness.
- Watch slippery floors.
- Warm up afterwards; do not combine with extreme heat or hard exercise
  immediately.
