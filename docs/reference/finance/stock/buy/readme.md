# 🛒 Stocks — buy

**What this is:** the long-only entry process — what to check before buying, the entry cases, position
sizing, and the facts that would invalidate the thesis. **Brazil, rules as of 2026 — verify current
tax, B3, and broker rules. Not financial advice.**

---

## ✅ Before buying

- **Thesis:** why this business, why now, and what the market is missing.
- **Valuation:** cheap against its own history and peers, not just cheap in absolute terms.
- **Balance sheet:** debt, maturities, interest coverage, dilution risk.
- **Cash:** operating cash flow and free cash flow, not only accounting profit.
- **Catalyst:** rate cut, margin recovery, new management, asset sale, cycle turn.
- **Invalidation:** the fact that would prove the thesis wrong.
- **Liquidity:** ADTV, free float, spread, and days to exit. If it fails the gate, skip it.
- **Size:** a position you can hold through a drawdown without forced selling.
- **Tax:** monthly sales volume, cost basis, loss credit, DARF timing.

## 🧩 Entry cases

<!-- begin table -->
| Case | Trigger | Action | Invalidate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Undervalued quality** | Low multiple vs history/peers; strong ROIC/FCF; low debt | Buy in 2–3 tranches; hold | Earnings fall; debt rises; moat breaks |
| **Turnaround** | Depressed margins; OCF inflection; net cash; rate cuts | Small starter; add on confirmation | Debt wall; dilution; no OCF recovery |
| **Growth at a reasonable price** | High growth; PEG below 1; positive FCF | Buy on pullback; size for volatility | Growth decelerates; multiple compresses |
| **Dividend / income** | Stable cash flow; sustainable payout | Buy for proventos; reinvest or spend | Payout unsustainable; JCP cut |
| **Cyclical** | Low P/E at peak earnings is a trap | Buy when the cycle turns, not at peak | Inventory builds; cycle peaks |
| **Macro-sensitive retailer** | Selic/DI falling; delinquency peaks; margin recovery | Wait for OCF and margin confirmation | Rates rise; credit worsens |
| **No buy / value trap** | Declining revenue; debt wall; dilution | Wait; do not average down | Thesis never confirms |
<!-- end table -->

### 🟢 Undervalued quality

A good business at a fair-to-cheap price. The multiple is below its own history or the peer group,
but the returns on capital and the cash flow are intact. Enter in tranches; do not spend the whole
budget on the first print.

### 🔧 Turnaround

The market has priced the bad cycle. The first sign is not the net income — it is operating cash flow,
gross margin, and net cash versus short-term debt. A small starter is enough until the operational
turn is visible.

### 🚀 Growth at a reasonable price

Growth can justify a higher multiple, but not any multiple. PEG below 1 is a rough filter, not a
promise. Size the position for volatility; a growth stock can halve while the thesis is still intact.

### 💵 Dividend / income

The payout must be covered by cash flow. A high yield with a collapsing business is a return **of**
capital, not **on** capital. Check the payout ratio, debt, and the JCP/dividend mix.

### 🔄 Cyclical

Low P/E at peak earnings is often the warning, not the bargain. Buy when the cycle is turning and
the market still hates the sector; sell when the cycle is obvious and the multiple looks “cheap” again.

### 🏬 Macro-sensitive retailer

Retailers with consumer credit and debt are sensitive to the Selic/DI curve, delinquency, and the
credit cycle. Watch operating cash flow, gross margin, EBITDA margin, SG&A dilution, and net cash
before adding. Magalu is the case study, not a recommendation.

## 🧱 Position sizing

<!-- begin table -->
| Question | Rule of thumb |
| --- | --- |
| Can I hold through a 50% drawdown? | If not, the position is too large |
| Is the position a meaningful part of the portfolio? | 5–15% is already concentrated for most people |
| Is the stock liquid enough to exit? | Position ≤ 20% of ADTV; lower is better |
| Is the thesis binary? | Size as if it can go to zero |
| Is there debt or dilution risk? | Smaller position; wait for confirmation |
| Is the expected return worth the research? | Compare with CDI after tax and risk |
<!-- end table -->

**Sleep test:** if the position keeps you awake, cut it until it does not. The best position size is
the one you can hold through the bad case without making a panic decision.

## 🧪 Entry checklist

1. What is the business, in one sentence?
2. Why is the price wrong today?
3. What is the bear/base/bull value per share?
4. What is the downside if the thesis fails?
5. Is the balance sheet strong enough to survive two bad years?
6. Is the cash flow real — FCF conversion, accruals, working capital?
7. Is the industry growing or shrinking?
8. Is the stock liquid enough to exit?
9. What would make me sell?
10. What is the maximum position size?

## 🚫 No-buy list

- A low P/E caused by one-off earnings.
- A low P/B with a low ROE and high debt.
- A high dividend yield funded by debt or dilution.
- A company that needs new capital to survive.
- A stock with low ADTV, low free float, or a recent reverse split.
- A thesis that depends on a rumour, a politician, or a single customer.
- A company you cannot explain in one paragraph.
