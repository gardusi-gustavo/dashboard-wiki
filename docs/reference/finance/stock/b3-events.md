# 🏛️ B3 events — corporate actions and market halts

**What this is:** the events that change the shares, the price, or your ability to trade — Data COM
and Data EX, dividends, JCP, splits, bonuses, subscriptions, auctions, circuit breakers, recalls,
and delisting. **Brazil, rules as of 2026 — verify current B3 and broker rules. Not financial
advice.**

---

## 📅 Data COM and Data EX

<!-- begin table -->
| Date | Meaning | Who gets the event |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Data COM** | “With” the event | Hold the stock on this date to receive it |
| **Data EX** | “Without” the event | Buy on this date and you do not receive it |
| **Payment date** | When the cash or shares arrive | Usually later than Data EX |
<!-- end table -->

The price normally adjusts on Data EX by the value of the event. A dividend is not free money: the
share price usually falls by roughly the payout on the ex-date.

## 💰 Dividends and JCP

- **Dividends:** paid from profit; as of 2026, 10% IRRF above R$50k/month to the same individual,
  under the rule described. Verify current law.
- **JCP:** interest on equity; 15% IRRF at source.
- **Reinvestment:** some brokers allow automatic reinvestment; check the price and fees.
- **Currency:** dividends are paid in BRL; foreign investors have different rules.

## 🧬 Splits, groupings, and bonuses

<!-- begin table -->
| Event | What changes | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Split** | More shares, lower price | 1 share at R$100 → 2 at R$50 |
| **Grouping** | Fewer shares, higher price | 10 shares at R$2 → 1 at R$20 |
| **Bonus** | New shares without payment | 1 bonus for every 10 held |
| **Subscription** | Right to buy new shares | Usually at a discount; the right has value |
<!-- end table -->

A split or bonus changes the number of shares and the cost basis per share, not the total cost basis.
A grouping can make a low-priced stock look “cleaner” without changing the business; it is also a
common feature of manipulated microcaps.

## 🧾 Subscription rights

- The right to subscribe is usually tradable for a short window.
- You can subscribe, sell the right, or let it expire worthless.
- Ignoring the right can dilute your position.
- The subscription price, ratio, and dates are in the company’s notice.

## 🔔 Auctions and circuit breakers

<!-- begin table -->
| Event | What happens | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Opening auction** | Orders accumulate before the open | The opening price can gap |
| **Closing auction** | Orders accumulate before the close | The close can differ from the last trade |
| **Volatility auction** | Trading pauses after a sharp move | You may not exit at the price on screen |
| **Circuit breaker** | Market-wide pause | Liquidity disappears exactly when you need it |
<!-- end table -->

A stop order is not a guarantee. In a halt, the stop cannot execute; the stock can reopen far below
or above it.

## 🩳 Short recalls and corporate actions

- The lender can recall borrowed shares; the broker may close your short.
- Dividends, JCP, splits, and bonuses change the buyback obligation.
- A subscription right may require action; ignoring it can create a loss.
- A merger, delisting, or judicial recovery can force a close at an uncertain price.

## ⚠️ Delisting and judicial recovery

- **Delisting:** the stock leaves the exchange; liquidity can collapse before the final date.
- **Judicial recovery:** the company restructures debt; equity can be diluted or wiped out.
- **Trading halt:** the stock may not trade for days or weeks.
- **Zero balance:** a short position can lose more than the collateral after a gap.

These are not rare edge cases in Brazil. Check the company’s filings and the broker’s event calendar.

## 🧪 Event checklist

1. Is there a Data COM/EX event in the next 30 days?
2. Is the dividend/JCP covered by cash flow?
3. Is there a split, bonus, or subscription pending?
4. Is the stock in an auction or halt?
5. Is the short borrow subject to recall?
6. Is there a delisting or recovery risk?
7. Does the event change the cost basis or the buyback obligation?
