# 📤 Stocks — sell and short

**What this is:** the exit side — when to sell, how to sell in pieces, what happens when the broker
forces the exit, and the separate short-selling operation. **Brazil, rules as of 2026 — verify
current tax, B3, and broker rules. Not financial advice.**

## 🚪 Exit checklist

<!-- begin table -->
| Trigger | Action | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Price above the expected range** | Sell in 2–3 tranches | Do not wait for the last cent |
| **Thesis broke** | Exit; do not average down | A lower price is not a better thesis |
| **Debt or dilution risk rose** | Reduce or exit | The equity can be diluted to zero |
| **Accounting quality fell** | Exit or demand evidence | Cash flow and accruals matter more than the headline |
| **Liquidity deteriorated** | Exit earlier, in smaller pieces | The exit is harder than the entry |
| **Better risk-adjusted opportunity** | Compare after tax and liquidity | Switching has a cost |
| **Position too large to hold** | Cut to the sleeping size | Forced selling is the real risk |
| **Short borrow or margin rising** | Close or hedge | The broker can close it for you |
<!-- end table -->

## 🧨 Why exits fail

- **Anchor on the purchase price.** The market does not know or care what you paid.
- **Wait for the old target.** The target was built on assumptions that may have changed.
- **Average down into a broken thesis.** A lower price is not a margin of safety if the business is
  impaired.
- **Ignore liquidity.** A wide spread and low ADTV can turn a small exit into a large loss.
- **Forget tax.** The gain after tax may be worse than the CDI alternative.
- **Hold through a margin call.** The broker does not wait for the thesis to recover.
