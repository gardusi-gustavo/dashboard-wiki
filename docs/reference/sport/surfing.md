# 🏄 Surfing — duck dives, depth, and the impact zone
**What this is:** a practical read on duck-diving heavy waves — how deep you need to go by wave
height, why depth does not scale linearly, the pendulum technique, and the failure rules that matter
more than pride. **Not a guarantee; the ocean always wins a brute-force contest.**
---
## 📏 Depth by wave height
Rules of thumb for a shortboard in a heavy beach break. “Peak” is the highest section just as the
wave breaks; the foam ball can go deeper than the face suggests.
<!-- begin table -->
| Wave face | Peak | Duck-dive depth | Feasibility | What it feels like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.5 m | ~0.8 m | 10–20 cm | very easy | minimal effort |
| 1.0 m | ~1.3 m | 20–30 cm | easy | basic arm/knee pressure |
| 1.5 m | ~1.8 m | 30–50 cm | moderate | timing + foot drive |
| 2.0 m | ~2.5 m | 50–80 cm | hard | heavy-swell limit; 30+ kg of force |
| 2.5 m | ~3.0 m | 80–120 cm | very hard | near the limit; the lip can rip the board |
| 3.0 m+ | ~3.5 m+ | >150 cm | impossible | turbulence deeper than you can submerge |
<!-- end table -->
The numbers are not linear. A plunging beach break can make the foam ball 1.5–2× deeper than a
rolling reef wave of the same face. The practical wall is **~3 m**.
---
## 🌊 Why depth does not scale linearly
Three different curves stack:
- **Wave energy:** `E ∝ H²` — a 3 m wave carries ~2.25× the energy of a 2 m wave.
- **Drag:** `F_d = ½ ρ v² C_d A` — doubling the water speed quadruples the force on the board.
- **Orbital motion:** the water rotates below the surface; the turbulence of a breaking wave is a
  volume, not a line.
A 25–30 L shortboard needs ~25–30 kg just to submerge. At 2 m+ the wave adds another 30+ kg; at 3 m+
it is no longer a technique problem — it is a force problem.

## 🫁 The calm rule
- **Lower the heart rate before the set.** Panic doubles oxygen consumption.
- **One clean, deep breath** before submerging. No hyperventilation.
- **Do not hold the board down forever.** Time the dive 1–2 m before the lip; deep green water is
  calm, the surface is chaos.
- **Commit, then relax.** Holding your breath while fighting the board drains the tank faster than
  the wave does.
---
## 🏹 The pendulum duck dive
The board is the shield. The body holds firm while the board rotates through the energy.
<!-- begin table -->
| Phase | Action | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Entry** | Weight forward, hands on the rails near the chest, nose 30–45° down | Slice under the energy instead of slamming into it |
| **Depth** | Drive the nose down; keep the body flush to the deck | A gap lets water rip the board out of your arms |
| **Bottom** | Board flat at maximum depth; foot/knee on the tail | The tail is the longest lever; the body is the anchor |
| **Exit** | Tail down, nose scoops up with buoyancy | Rebound through the back of the wave |
<!-- end table -->
Lock your arms against your chest so the skeleton takes the load, not the biceps. If the board goes
through, you go through — the body just needs to stay attached.
---
## 🛟 When it fails
- **If the lip is slamming directly on you:** bail deep, cover your head, let the leash take the
  pull. Do not try to hold the board through the impact.
- **3-attempt rule:** if three duck dives in a row fail to make progress, turn around and ride the
  whitewater back to shore.
- **The last waves of a set are taller and heavier.** Do not fight them on an empty tank.
- **If you make it out:** sit up, face the ocean, and rest 5–10 minutes before paddling into
  anything. The lineup is not going anywhere.
- **Insisting is the risk.** Returning to shore is the skill.
---
## 🌊 Big waves — the 3 m wall
Above ~3 m, duck diving is effectively over:
- **Guns** — longer, heavier boards; not duck-divable.
- **Tow-in / jetski** — bypass the impact zone; pick up the surfer if caught inside.
- **Channels** — spend the energy getting around the break, not through it.
- **Safety vests** — CO₂ inflation for deep hold-downs.
- **Bail deep** — if the board is going to be ripped away, let it go and protect your head.
At that size, success is mostly positioning and ocean reading, not arm strength.
---
## 📍 Heavy beach-break note
Heavy beach breaks can get short-period on a strong south/southeast swell. A deep-to-shallow trench
can make a 2 m swell feel like a 3 m peak.
- **Plunging waves** (dumpers) drive the foam ball deeper than a rolling reef break.
- A 2 m face with a 3 m peak is already at the heavy-swell limit for a normal duck dive.
- If the sets keep coming, use the channel or go back to the sand. The next set is not worth the
  last breath.
