# 🧯 Redundancy
> **Updated:** 2026-10-26

**What this is:** how many of a thing to own — a spare where failure is costly, one where replacement is trivial.

## ⚖️ The rule

<!-- begin table -->
| Signal | Keep |
| --- | --- |
| Failure is costly or slow to undo (phone, boot drive) | a spare |
| Durable and trivially replaced (fork, mug) | exactly one |
| Wear item with a clear replacement signal | one, replaced on wear |
<!-- end table -->

## 📱 Spares

- **Two phones** — one street device, one secure; either can cover the other.
- **Two boot drives** — Linux and Windows recovery media.
- **Cold copies** — keep at least one encrypted copy off the machine.

## 🍴 Singles

Simple, durable items earn no spare: one fork, one plate, one bowl, one spoon, one small knife. A second copy is clutter, not resilience.

Kitchen singles are deliberate: **one pressure cooker**, **one stove or portable hot plate**, **one fridge (unit-provided)**, and **one blender if bought**. No spare appliance. A spare is only for failure that is costly or slow to undo; a missing fork is neither.

## 🔁 Replacement over spares

Buy cheap, replace on wear, and donate/leave at move-out. The "Own?" and "Replacement signal" columns in the domain notes are the trigger to replace, not to stockpile.
